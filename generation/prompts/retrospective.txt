SEEDING BRIEF: retrospective
============================
Read with the `write-seed-segments` skill, which holds the shared labelling rules. This brief
only adds what is specific to retrospectives.

THE MEETING
- 4–8 people, 500–900 words, after a sprint, launch, incident or quarter. A facilitator (S1)
  runs it; the whole team speaks.
- Structure: what went well, what didn't, what we change. Ends with a short list of
  improvement actions with owners.

REGISTER
- Reflective and conversational, some emotion ("honestly that week was rough"), people build
  on each other's points, more speakers per segment than other types.

WHAT EACH ZONE LOOKS LIKE HERE
- context: what we're looking back on and the format. "We're looking back at the {PROJECT}
  launch, went well first, then what to change." (1 for the scope; 0 for format chatter).
- discussion: observations. Important (1): a concrete problem or root cause, a measured
  outcome, a recurring pain point ("Three of the five incidents came from config changes.").
  Chatter (0): agreement, venting without content, "+1", jokes, "yeah, same".
- decision: agreed process changes. "From next sprint, every config change needs a second
  reviewer." (1). Murmurs of agreement (decision, 0).
- action_item: improvement actions. "{S5|owner} will draft the new checklist by
  {DATE_REL|deadline}." (1). Retro action items often have a team owner: "{TEAM|owner}".
- closing: thanks, and when the actions will be reviewed (1 if dated).

GOOD SEGMENT SHAPES
- Several people adding to one theme (4–5 speakers), one important root-cause line.
- A positive observation that is important because it should be kept ("Pairing on reviews cut
  our turnaround to {NUMBER} hours.").
- A change proposed, refined by someone else, then agreed (only the agreed version is 1).

AVOID
- Every complaint being important: venting is chatter unless it names a concrete problem.
- Blame aimed at named people; retros talk about process.
