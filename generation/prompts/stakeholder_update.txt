SEEDING BRIEF: stakeholder_update
=================================
Read with the `write-seed-segments` skill, which holds the shared labelling rules. This brief
only adds what is specific to stakeholder updates.

THE MEETING
- 3–6 people, 450–750 words. A team lead (S1) updates senior stakeholders, a sponsor or a
  client-facing manager. Stakeholders ask pointed questions and make high-level calls.
- Topics: progress vs commitments, budget ({MONEY}), timelines ({DATE_ABS}), risks needing
  escalation, asks ("we need a decision on…").
- Decisions are high-level: approve budget, accept a delay, change priority, sign off scope.

REGISTER
- Polished and summary-level: "Overall we're on track", "the headline is". Less jargon.
- Stakeholders speak briefly and decisively; the presenter speaks in longer turns.

WHAT EACH ZONE LOOKS LIKE HERE
- context: purpose and headline. "The goal today is to get your sign-off on the {PRODUCT}
  launch date." (1). Pleasantries (0).
- discussion: headline facts and asks. Important (1): status against commitments, slips,
  budget variance, escalated risks, the explicit ask. Chatter (0): polite framing ("as you
  know"), positive filler ("the team has been great"), clarifying questions with no new fact.
- decision: usually made by a stakeholder (S2/S3), not the presenter. "Approved, take the
  extra {MONEY} from the contingency." (1). "Thanks, that's helpful." (decision, 0).
- action_item: mostly the presenter taking follow-ups, sometimes a stakeholder offering help.
  "{S1|owner} will send the revised plan to the steering group by {DATE_REL|deadline}." (1).
- closing: next update date (1), thanks (0).

GOOD SEGMENT SHAPES
- Presenter states a fact, stakeholder probes once, presenter answers with a number (1).
- An ask followed by a stakeholder decision, with one condition attached.
- Deferred decisions: "Let me think about it and come back to you by {DATE_REL}." is an action
  item for the stakeholder (1), not a decision.

AVOID
- Engineering detail: stakeholders get the summary.
- Labelling every presenter sentence important: much of it is framing.
