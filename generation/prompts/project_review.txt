SEEDING BRIEF: project_review
=============================
Read with the `write-seed-segments` skill, which holds the shared labelling rules. This brief
only adds what is specific to project reviews.

THE MEETING
- 4–7 people, 600–1,100 words, a scheduled review of one project or milestone, chaired by a
  lead (S1). Leads, specialists and sometimes a manager attend.
- Agenda-driven: status vs plan, risks, open questions, then explicit decisions and owners.
- Decision-heavy: the typical review makes 2–4 decisions and assigns several owners.

REGISTER
- Semi-formal, longer turns, reasoning out loud, trade-offs and numbers ({PERCENT}, {MONEY},
  {NUMBER}, {METRIC}, {DATE_ABS}).
- Disagreement is normal; decisions are stated explicitly by the chair or the owner.

WHAT EACH ZONE LOOKS LIKE HERE
- context: agenda and goal. "Today we review {PROJECT} against the {DATE_ABS} milestone and
  decide on scope." (1). "Let's wait a minute for {PERSON}." (0).
- discussion: trade-offs, risks, evidence. Important (1): risks, key numbers, dependencies,
  findings ("Error rate is at {PERCENT}, above our {PERCENT} target."). Chatter (0): framing,
  rhetorical questions, "good point", restating what someone just said.
- decision: options weighed, then a clear call. Exactly one sentence carries the decision as
  importance 1; deliberation before it is discussion; acknowledgements after it are decision, 0.
  If the decision is restated a turn later, only the first, clearest statement is 1.
- action_item: owners for follow-ups from the decisions. "{S4|owner} will update the risk log
  by {DATE_REL|deadline}." (1).
- closing: recap and next review. The recap repeats decisions, so it is 0; the date of the
  next review is 1.

GOOD SEGMENT SHAPES
- 5–10 sentences, 3–4 speakers, with a real disagreement before the decision.
- A decision with a condition ("We go ahead, but only if the audit passes.") is still 1.
- A discussion segment with two important facts and four chatter lines.

AVOID
- Decisions that come out of nowhere: give one or two lines of reasoning.
- Vague closings that restate everything; keep closings short.
